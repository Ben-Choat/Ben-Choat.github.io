Reservoir Manager

You are the water resource manager for a reservoir serving a growing city, farms, and a hydroelectric facility. Your job: balance water supply, flood safety, and downstream needs over a full 52-week water year.

Each week you receive probabilistic forecasts (95% CI) for both inflow and demand. Decide how much to release — then actuals are revealed and storage updates. Units are abstract (think thousand acre-feet).

ZoneStorageConsequence
Dead Pool 0 – 100 Water unreachable by gravity. −60 pts/wk; if storage falls below 50, an additional −30 pts "critical drought" penalty applies.
Conservation Pool 100 – 600 Target operating zone. +10 pts/wk base, plus up to +12 proximity bonus for being near the target of 350.
Flood Control Pool 600 – 850 Reserved empty capacity to absorb flood pulses. 0 pts/wk — not a crisis, but wasted scoring opportunity.
Surcharge Pool 850 – 1,000 ⚠ GAME OVER. The dam overtops and the downstream city is destroyed. Do not let storage reach this zone.

Downstream demands — your weekly release must cover all of these or you incur a shortfall penalty (~−1 pt per 4 units short, minimum −2 pts):

DemandSeasonTypical range
Urban water supplyPeaks in summer (wk 30)5 – 15 units/wk
Agriculture (irrigation)Peaks early summer (wk 26), near zero in winter0 – 25 units/wk
HydroelectricPeaks in winter (wk 4)2 – 14 units/wk
Environmental flowBaseflow year-round; spawning and migration pulses13 – 27 units/wk

Environmental-flow regime: every week includes 9 units of baseflow to maintain habitat and 4 units of subsistence flow for connected refuges and downstream basic needs. Short, higher pulse flows are scheduled for fish spawning (weeks 15–20) and bird migration (weeks 10–13 and 40–44); their timing is known in the forecast, while the needed pulse magnitude has modest uncertainty.

Flood watches: when the inflow forecast signals a major storm, a flood card shows the chance of reaching the flood-control pool and the downstream safe-release limit. During these events, storm runoff replaces scheduled environmental baseflow. Finish in the conservation pool without exceeding the channel limit to earn a flood-operation bonus; excessive releases flood the valley, while surcharge storage still ends the game.

Rationing is sometimes the right call — draining storage to dead pool costs far more than a modest shortfall penalty. But chronic under-delivery is expensive too.

Tip: If snowmelt is enabled, spring inflow (weeks 14–26) includes a runoff surge that peaks in mid-spring. Capture enough for summer demand, while preserving flood space if a storm arrives on top of the melt.
